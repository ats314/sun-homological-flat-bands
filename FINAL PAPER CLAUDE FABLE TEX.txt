\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We study the Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian of pure $\SU(N)$ gauge theory on a periodic cubic
lattice, in strong-coupling perturbation theory on the charge-conjugation-odd one-plaquette
shell. Charge-odd plaquette states are oriented $2$-chains, and we prove that the
second-order effective Hamiltonian is $t_N$ times the down Laplacian
$\Ldn=\partial_2\dg\partial_2$ of the cubical complex, with
$t_N=2N(N^2-4)/[(N^2-1)(2N^2-1)(4N^2-9)]$. The spectrum on each Bloch fibre is
$\{0,q,q\}$; the flat band is $\ker\partial_2$, of exact dimension $L^3+2$ on the
$L^3$ torus, spanned by cube boundaries and three wrapping sheets, and it touches the
dispersive doublet singularly at $\Gamma$. We then prove a centre-parity theorem: for even
$N$ every odd order of the expansion vanishes identically, and for odd $N$ nothing survives
below order $N-2$, where the only term is a one-plaquette vertex
$-(N/(N+1))^{N-3}/((N-3)!)^2$. Hence the band is exactly flat through third order for every
$N\ge 4$; for $\SU(3)$ the same holds by exact computation. At fourth order, exact
computation over the field $\Q(N)$ gives every cluster cumulant as a single rational
function of $N$; the kernel is a polynomial in the two Hodge Laplacians plus one further
operator, which forces two of the four symmetry-allowed shape coefficients to vanish, and
the band acquires the dispersion
$[\alpha_N\sum_i X_i^2+\beta_N\sum_{i<j}X_iX_j]/(2\sum_i X_i)$, $X_i=1-\cos k_i$, with
$\alpha_N=640/[N(N^2-1)^3]$ and $\beta_N$ an explicit rational function. We prove
$\beta_N>\alpha_N>0$ for all $N\ge3$, so the minimum is at $\Gamma$, the maximum at the
zone corner, and the bandwidth is $\alpha_N+\beta_N$. Finally, the one-plaquette problem is
$N$ free fermions in a cosine potential minus a centre-of-mass term; we prove that in the
$\U(N)$ model the fundamental doublet does not split below order $N-1$, which turns an
observed cancellation between irreducible channels into a theorem at every even order and
yields the bound $O(N^{-(m+1)})$ on the order-$m$ splitting. The observed exponent is
$N^{-(2m-1)}$; we state what remains to prove it. The fourth-order results are computer
assisted and rest on four assumptions that we state explicitly. All statements concern a
fixed lattice spacing; none is a statement about the continuum limit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Flat bands --- dispersion branches that are constant over the whole Brillouin zone ---
are usually engineered. They arise from destructive interference on line graphs and
related frustrated geometries \cite{Sutherland1986,Lieb1989,Mielke1991,Tasaki1992}, they
carry compact localised states, and when they touch a dispersive band the touching is
forced by real-space topology \cite{Bergman2008,RhimYang2019}. A unifying algebraic
description is that the hopping matrix is a Gram matrix $MM\dg$ of a rectangular incidence
matrix $M$, and the flat band is $\ker M\dg$ \cite{Kollar2020,Roychowdhury2024}. Closed
two-dimensional surfaces play the same role in $2$-form spin liquids
\cite{ChungGingras2025}, and a recent construction obtains extensive face-graph degeneracy
from local graph topology \cite{Hazra2026}.

This paper shows that the same structure appears, with no tuning, in the
strong-coupling expansion of non-Abelian lattice gauge theory. In the Hamiltonian
formulation \cite{KS1975} the lowest excitations at strong coupling are single plaquettes
of fundamental flux. The charge-conjugation-odd combination changes sign when the
orientation of the plaquette is reversed, so the charge-odd one-plaquette shell is
canonically the space $C_2$ of $2$-chains of the spatial cubical complex. Group
integration over the link shared by two neighbouring plaquettes then produces a hopping
amplitude whose sign is the product of the two incidence numbers. The second-order
effective Hamiltonian is therefore a multiple of $\partial_2\dg\partial_2$, and its zero
modes are the $2$-cycles.

\subsection{Results}

We separate statements \emph{proved} in the text from statements obtained by
\emph{exact computation}, which are labelled ``Computed result'' and come with their
assumptions.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Second order (proved; Section~\ref{sec:second}).} On the charge-odd
  shell, $H_2=c_2(N)\,I+t_N\,\Ldn$ with $t_N$ as in the abstract
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:second}). On each Bloch fibre $\Ldn(k)=q(k)I-w(k)w(k)\dg$ with
  spectrum $\{0,q,q\}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:bloch}). The flat band has dimension
  $L^3+2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:carrier}) and its Bloch vector is discontinuous at $\Gamma$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:singular}).
\item \textbf{Odd orders (proved; Section~\ref{sec:odd}).} The centre-parity theorem
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}) and its closed-form vertex. Corollary: the band is flat
  through third order for every $N\ge4$. For $\SU(3)$ the third-order operator is
  $\tfrac{1975}{124848}\,\Ldn$ plus a scalar (Computed result~\ref{res:third}).
\item \textbf{Fourth order (computed, with proved consequences;
  Section~\ref{sec:fourth}).} The kernel is a Hodge polynomial plus one operator
  (Computed result~\ref{res:hodge}); consequently two shape coefficients vanish
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}, proved from the form). All cumulants are rational functions
  of $N$ and $\beta_N$ is assembled from three of them (Computed result~\ref{res:closed},
  Theorem~\ref{thm:beta}). The band extrema and bandwidth follow
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:extrema}, proved from the closed forms), as does a single angular
  invariant governing the coupling of the flat band to the dispersive doublet
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:mixing}).
\item \textbf{One plaquette as free fermions (proved; Section~\ref{sec:fermion}).} The
  $\U(N)$ doublet does not split below order $N-1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:nosplit}); the
  $\SU(N)$ splitting is generated entirely by the centre-of-mass term and is
  $O(N^{-(m+1)})$ at order $m$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:grading}). The stronger observed law
  is Conjecture~\ref{conj:grading}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}

Hamiltonian strong-coupling series for glueball masses were developed in
\cite{KSS1976,Hamer1989} and, in the Euclidean formulation, in \cite{Munster1981}; they
are series for energies at zero momentum. After conversion between normalisations, the
$\Gamma$-point coefficients of the charge-odd shell obtained here agree through third
order with the $1^{+-}$ series of \cite{Hamer1989}. What is new here is the momentum
dependence, its identification with the Laplacian of the cell complex, and the closed
forms in $N$. Group integrals are evaluated with the Weingarten calculus
\cite{Weingarten1978,CollinsSniady2006}. The representation of one-plaquette class
functions by free fermions on a circle is classical \cite{GrossWitten1980,Wadia1980,
MinahanPolychronakos1993,Douglas1993}; our use of it, to prove a cancellation in
perturbation theory, appears to be new. Discrete Hodge theory on cell complexes goes back
to \cite{Eckmann1944}. Truncated plaquette bases of this kind are also the starting point
of current proposals for quantum simulation \cite{CBB2025,Balaji2026}.

\subsection{What is not claimed}

Every result is a statement about an effective operator of degenerate perturbation
theory on a finite-dimensional shell at fixed lattice spacing and finite volume.
Convergence of the expansion is not addressed beyond the isolation of the shell
(Proposition~\ref{prop:isolation}), and nothing here bears on the continuum limit: along
an asymptotically free trajectory the coupling $u$ of \eqref{eq:H} tends to infinity,
outside any strong-coupling regime.

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Hamiltonian and normalisation}

Let $\Lambda=(\Z/L\Z)^3$ with $L\ge3$, with oriented links $\ell=(x,\mu)$ carrying
$U_\ell\in\SU(N)$, $N\ge3$, and Hilbert space $\bigotimes_\ell L^2(\SU(N),dU_\ell)$ with
Haar measure. Write $C_\ell$ for the quadratic Casimir operator of link $\ell$,
normalised so that on the fundamental representation $F$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CF}
\begin{aligned}
  C_2(F)&=\CF=\frac{N^2-1}{2N}, & C_2(\Adj)&=N,\\
  C_2(\Lambda^2F)&=\frac{(N+1)(N-2)}{N}, & C_2(\Sym^2F)&=\frac{(N-1)(N+2)}{N}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We study
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
  H=H_E-u\,W,\qquad H_E=\frac12\sum_\ell C_\ell,\qquad
  W=\sum_{p}\bigl(\Tr U_p+\Tr U_p\dg\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over unoriented plaquettes and $U_p$ is the ordered product of the four
link variables around $p$. If the Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian is written
$aH_{\mathrm{KS}}=\tfrac{g^2}{2}\sum_\ell C_\ell-\tfrac{1}{g^2}W$, then $H=aH_{\mathrm{KS}}/g^2$
and $u=g^{-4}$. Physical states are gauge invariant. Charge conjugation
$\mathcal C:U_\ell\mapsto U_\ell^{*}$ commutes with $H$, as do lattice translations, the
cubic group, and the centre symmetry that multiplies all links crossing a fixed plane by
an element of $\Z_N$.

\subsection{The charge-odd shell}

At $u=0$ the ground state is the constant function $|0\rangle$, with $H_E|0\rangle=0$. For
an oriented plaquette $p$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shell}
  |p\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigl(\Tr U_p-\Tr U_p\dg\bigr)|0\rangle .
\end{equation}
These states are orthonormal by character orthogonality, have $H_E|p\rangle=2\CF|p\rangle$
(four fundamental links at energy $\CF/2$ each), are odd under $\mathcal C$, and satisfy
$|{-p}\rangle=-|p\rangle$, where $-p$ is $p$ with reversed orientation. The last property is
the point of the construction: \emph{the map $p\mapsto|p\rangle$ identifies the span
$\mathcal P$ of these states with the space $C_2$ of cellular $2$-chains}, of dimension
$3L^3$.

\begin{proposition}[isolation]\label{prop:isolation}
In the gauge-invariant, $\mathcal C$-odd sector of trivial centre charge, the spectrum of
$H_E$ in $[0,2\CF+\gamma_N)$ is $\{2\CF\}$, with eigenspace $\mathcal P$, where
$\gamma_N=\CF$ for $N=3$ and $N\ge5$, and $\gamma_4=5/4$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Eigenstates of $H_E$ are labelled by an irreducible representation on each link, and the
energy is $\tfrac12\sum_\ell C_2(\rho_\ell)$; the smallest nonzero Casimir is $\CF$. Gauge
invariance forbids a vertex with exactly one excited link, so the excited links form a
subgraph with all degrees at least two. In the cubic lattice with $L\ge3$ such a subgraph
with trivial centre charge has at least four links, and with exactly four links it is a
plaquette; at a two-valent vertex the two representations must be conjugate along the
path, so a plaquette state is $\chi_\rho(U_p)|0\rangle$ with energy $2C_2(\rho)$. The
values $\rho=F,\bar F$ give the shell and its $\mathcal C$-even partner. The remaining
plaquette states have $2C_2(\rho)-2\CF\ge 2C_2(\Lambda^2F)-2\CF=(N-3)(N+1)/N$ for $N\ge4$
and $\ge 2C_2(\Adj)-2\CF=10/3$ for $N=3$, where $\Lambda^2F=\bar F$. There is no admissible
subgraph with five links; with six or more links the energy is at least $3\CF$. A
Polyakov line in a representation of zero $N$-ality has energy at least $3N/2>3\CF$.
Comparing $(N-3)(N+1)/N$ with $\CF$ gives $\gamma_N$.
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:isolation} and the boundedness of $W$ at finite volume, analytic
perturbation theory applies to the shell. We write $P$ for the projector onto $\mathcal P$,
$Q=1-P$, $\mathcal R=Q(2\CF-H_E)^{-1}Q$, and use the Hermitian (des Cloizeaux) effective
Hamiltonian \cite{Bloch1958,desCloizeaux1960}
\begin{gather*}
  H_{\mathrm{eff}}=2\CF+\sum_{m\ge1}u^mH_m,\qquad H_1=-PWP,\qquad H_2=PW\mathcal RWP,\\
  H_3=-\bigl(PW\mathcal RW\mathcal RWP-\tfrac12\{PW\mathcal R^2WP,\,PWP\}\bigr),
\end{gather*}
with energies measured from the perturbed vacuum energy. By translation and cubic
symmetry the diagonal of every $H_m$ is a scalar.

\section{Second order: the down Laplacian}\label{sec:second}

\subsection{The cell complex}

Let $C_0,C_1,C_2,C_3$ be the real vector spaces on vertices, links, plaquettes and cubes,
with cell bases orthonormal, and boundary maps
$\partial_2[x;\mu\nu]=[x;\mu]+[x+\hat\mu;\nu]-[x+\hat\nu;\mu]-[x;\nu]$ and
$\partial_3[x]=\sum_{\mu}(-1)^{\mu-1}\bigl([x+\hat\mu;\mu^c]-[x;\mu^c]\bigr)$, where
$\mu^c$ is the complementary pair in increasing order. Then $\partial_2\partial_3=0$. On
$C_2$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplacians}
  \Ldn=\partial_2\dg\partial_2,\qquad \Lup=\partial_3\partial_3\dg,\qquad \Ldn\Lup=\Lup\Ldn=0 .
\end{equation}
The matrix element $(\Ldn)_{pp'}=\sum_\ell[p:\ell][p':\ell]$ is $4$ for $p=p'$, and for
$p\ne p'$ sharing a link it is $+1$ if the two plaquettes induce the same orientation on
that link and $-1$ otherwise. We write $\Ssq=\Ldn-4I$ for the signed shared-link adjacency.

\subsection{The hopping amplitude}

\begin{lemma}[shared-link channels]\label{lem:channels}
Let $p\ne p'$ share the link $\ell$, and let $\chi,\chi'\in\{\chi_F,\chi_{\bar F}\}$. The
product $\chi(U_p)\chi'(U_{p'})|0\rangle$ has unit norm. It decomposes according to the
irreducible representation $\rho$ carried by $\ell$, with $\rho\in\{\mathbf 1,\Adj\}$ if the
two fluxes traverse $\ell$ in opposite directions and $\rho\in\{\Lambda^2F,\Sym^2F\}$ if in
the same direction. The component in channel $\rho$ has squared norm $d_\rho/N^2$,
$d_\rho=\dim\rho$, and is an eigenstate of $H_E$ with energy $3\CF+\tfrac12C_2(\rho)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $U_p=U_\ell A$ and $U_{p'}=U_\ell^{\pm1}B$, where $A$ and $B$ are products of the three
private links of each plaquette and are independent Haar variables. Integrating over $A$
and $B$ with $\int A_{ij}\overline{A_{kl}}\,dA=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}/N$ gives, for the
projector $\Pi_\rho$ acting on the two copies of the representation space of $\ell$,
$\|\Pi_\rho\chi\chi'\|^2=N^{-2}\Tr\Pi_\rho=d_\rho/N^2$; summing over $\rho$ gives $1$. The six
private links are fundamental and $\ell$ carries $\rho$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[second order]\label{thm:second}
For every $N\ge3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H2}
  H_2=c_2(N)\,I+t_N\,\Ldn,\qquad
  t_N=\frac{2N(N^2-4)}{(N^2-1)(2N^2-1)(4N^2-9)}>0 ,
\end{equation}
where $c_2(N)$ is a scalar.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $p\ne p'$. A second-order path from $|p\rangle$ to $|p'\rangle$ either annihilates $p$
and then creates $p'$, through the vacuum, or creates $p'$ and then annihilates $p$. For
flux labels $\chi$ on $p$ and $\chi'$ on $p'$, the vacuum route contributes $1/(2\CF)$
independently of $(\chi,\chi')$. In the $\mathcal C$-odd combination \eqref{eq:shell} the
amplitude is
$\tfrac12[(F\!\to\!F')+(\bar F\!\to\!\bar F')-(F\!\to\!\bar F')-(\bar F\!\to\!F')]$, so every
contribution independent of $(\chi,\chi')$ cancels. If $p$ and $p'$ share no link, the
intermediate state of the second route is a product state of energy $4\CF$ and its
contribution is again independent of the labels; hence $H_2$ connects only plaquettes that
share a link.

Let $p,p'$ share $\ell$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:channels} the second route contributes
$\sum_\rho w_\rho$ over the two channels allowed by the relative direction, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
  w_\rho=\frac{d_\rho/N^2}{2\CF-\bigl(3\CF+\tfrac12C_2(\rho)\bigr)}
        =-\frac{d_\rho/N^2}{\CF+\tfrac12C_2(\rho)} .
\end{equation}
With \eqref{eq:CF},
\begin{align*}
  w_{\mathbf 1}&=-\frac{2}{N(N^2-1)}, & w_{\Adj}&=-\frac{2(N^2-1)}{N(2N^2-1)},\\
  w_{\Lambda^2}&=-\frac{N-1}{(N+1)(2N-3)}, & w_{\Sym^2}&=-\frac{N+1}{(N-1)(2N+3)} ,
\end{align*}
and the two sums are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sums}
  \Sigma^{\mathrm o}_N=w_{\mathbf 1}+w_{\Adj}=-\frac{2N^3}{(N^2-1)(2N^2-1)},\qquad
  \Sigma^{\mathrm s}_N=w_{\Lambda^2}+w_{\Sym^2}=-\frac{4N(N^2-2)}{(N^2-1)(4N^2-9)} .
\end{equation}
Suppose $p$ and $p'$ induce the same orientation on $\ell$. Then $(F\to F')$ and
$(\bar F\to\bar F')$ run through the same-direction channels and the two mixed amplitudes
through the opposite-direction channels, so the $\mathcal C$-odd amplitude is
$\Sigma^{\mathrm s}_N-\Sigma^{\mathrm o}_N$. If the induced orientations are opposite the
roles are exchanged and the amplitude is $\Sigma^{\mathrm o}_N-\Sigma^{\mathrm s}_N$. In
both cases it equals $t_N[p:\ell][p':\ell]$ with
\[
  t_N=\Sigma^{\mathrm s}_N-\Sigma^{\mathrm o}_N
     =\frac{2N}{N^2-1}\cdot\frac{N^2(4N^2-9)-2(N^2-2)(2N^2-1)}{(2N^2-1)(4N^2-9)}
     =\frac{2N(N^2-4)}{(N^2-1)(2N^2-1)(4N^2-9)} .
\]
Hence the off-diagonal part of $H_2$ is $t_N\Ssq$; the diagonal is a scalar, and absorbing
$-4t_N$ into it gives \eqref{eq:H2}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:tN}
(i) $t_3=5/612$. (ii) The formula vanishes at $N=2$, where $\chi_F$ is real and the shell
\eqref{eq:shell} is empty. (iii) As $N\to\infty$,
$t_N=\tfrac{1}{4}N^{-3}-\tfrac{1}{16}N^{-5}-\tfrac{77}{64}N^{-7}+O(N^{-9})$, although
$\Sigma^{\mathrm o}_N$ and $\Sigma^{\mathrm s}_N$ are each $-N^{-1}+O(N^{-3})$: one order in
$N^{-2}$ cancels between the two orientation classes. This is the first instance of the
pattern discussed in Section~\ref{sec:fermion}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bloch form, flat band, and multiplicity}

Order the face orbitals as $(12),(13),(23)$ and the link orbitals as $1,2,3$, and put
$d_j=e^{ik_j}-1$, $a_j=|d_j|^2=4\sin^2(k_j/2)$, $q=a_1+a_2+a_3$. The coboundary
$\partial_2\dg$ has Bloch matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
  \delta(k)=\begin{pmatrix} d_2&-d_1&0\\ d_3&0&-d_1\\ 0&d_3&-d_2\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad w(k)=\bigl(\bar d_3,\,-\bar d_2,\,\bar d_1\bigr)^{T},
\end{equation}
and $w(k)$ is the Bloch matrix of $\partial_3$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bloch}
$\Ldn(k)=\delta(k)\delta(k)\dg=q(k)I_3-w(k)w(k)\dg$ and $\Lup(k)=w(k)w(k)\dg$, so that
$\Ldn(k)+\Lup(k)=q(k)I_3$. For $q(k)>0$ the spectrum of $\Ldn(k)$ is $\{0,q,q\}$, with
$\ker\Ldn(k)=\C\,w(k)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The diagonal entries of $\delta\delta\dg$ are $a_1+a_2$, $a_1+a_3$, $a_2+a_3$, that is
$q-|w_i|^2$. The off-diagonal entries are $d_2\bar d_3$, $-d_1\bar d_3$ and $d_1\bar d_2$,
which equal $-w_1\bar w_2$, $-w_1\bar w_3$ and $-w_2\bar w_3$. Since $w\dg w=q$, the vector
$w$ is a null vector and $w^\perp$ is the $q$-eigenspace.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[multiplicity]\label{thm:carrier}
$\dim\ker\partial_2=(L^3-1)+3=L^3+2$. The kernel is the orthogonal sum of $\im\partial_3$,
spanned by the boundaries of the $L^3$ elementary cubes subject to the single relation
$\sum_x\partial_3[x]=0$, and the three-dimensional space of harmonic $2$-chains, spanned
modulo boundaries by the wrapping sheets $S_\mu=\sum_{x_\mu=0}[x;\mu^c]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Hodge decomposition of a finite complex \cite{Eckmann1944},
$\ker\partial_2=\im\partial_3\oplus\mathcal H_2$ with $\mathcal H_2\cong H_2(T^3;\R)$, of
dimension $b_2=3$. Since there are no $4$-cells, $\dim\ker\partial_3=b_3=1$, so
$\dim\im\partial_3=L^3-1$; the kernel of $\partial_3$ is spanned by the sum of all cubes,
because each plaquette lies in two cubes with opposite induced orientation. Each $S_\mu$ is
a cycle and the three are independent in homology. As a check, the Bloch fibres contribute
one null vector for each of the $L^3-1$ momenta with $q>0$ and three at $\Gamma$, where
$\Ldn(0)=0$.
\end{proof}

Each cube boundary is supported on six plaquettes and is annihilated by $\Ldn$: it is a
compact localised state. Figure~\ref{fig:bands} shows the spectrum.

\begin{proposition}[singular touching]\label{prop:singular}
For a unit vector $\hat n$, $\lim_{t\to0^+}w(t\hat n)/\sqrt{q(t\hat n)}=-i\,(n_3,-n_2,n_1)^T$.
The normalised Bloch vector of the flat band therefore has no limit at $\Gamma$: the
touching is singular in the sense of \cite{RhimYang2019}, which is the Bloch-space form of
the fact that the compact localised states do not span $\ker\partial_2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\bar d_j=-ik_j+O(k^2)$ and $q=|k|^2+O(k^4)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Odd orders: the centre-parity theorem}\label{sec:odd}

A matrix element of $H_m$ between shell states is a sum of products of factors
\[
  \langle a|\,W\mathcal R^{j_1}W\cdots\mathcal R^{j_{n-1}}W\,|b\rangle ,\qquad a,b\in\mathcal P,
\]
whose numbers $n$ of insertions of $W$ add up to $m$. Expanding $W$ and the two shell states in traces, a factor
with $n$ insertions is a sum of Haar integrals of products of $n+2$ \emph{face words}
$\Tr U_p^{\pm1}$, weighted by resolvent denominators.

\begin{theorem}[centre parity]\label{thm:parity}
Consider the expansion on $\Z^3$, or on the torus with $L$ even, or on any torus for
$m+2<L$.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item If $N$ is even, $H_m=0$ for every odd $m$.
\item If $N$ is odd, $H_m=0$ for every odd $m<N-2$, and $H_{N-2}$ is diagonal in the
  plaquette label, with
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:vertex}
    \bigl\langle\chi_{\bar F}(U_p)\bigr|H_{N-2}\bigl|\chi_F(U_p)\bigr\rangle
      =-\Bigl(\frac{N}{N+1}\Bigr)^{N-3}\frac{1}{\bigl((N-3)!\bigr)^2} .
  \end{equation}
  On the $\mathcal C$-odd shell $H_{N-2}=+\bigl(\tfrac{N}{N+1}\bigr)^{N-3}((N-3)!)^{-2}\,I$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Invariance of Haar measure under $U_\ell\mapsto zU_\ell$, $z\in\Z_N$, shows that an integral
of face words vanishes unless the net flux $f_\ell$ (forward minus backward traversals) is
$0$ modulo $N$ on every link. Let $T$ be the set of $x$-links at even $y$, $y$-links at
even $z$ and $z$-links at even $x$. Every plaquette meets $T$ in an odd number of links: an
$xy$-plaquette contains two $x$-links at consecutive values of $y$, exactly one of which is
in $T$, and two $y$-links at the same $z$; the other planes are alike. If $n_\ell$ is the
total number of traversals of $\ell$, then $n_\ell\equiv f_\ell$ modulo $2$, and
\[
  \#\{\text{face words}\}\;\equiv\;\sum_{\ell\in T}n_\ell\;\equiv\;\sum_{\ell\in T}f_\ell
  \pmod 2 .
\]
(1) If $N$ is even every $f_\ell$ is even, so a nonvanishing factor has an even number
$n+2$ of face words. If $m$ is odd, every term of $H_m$ contains a factor with $n$ odd.

(2) Let $N$ be odd and consider a nonvanishing factor with $n$ odd. Some $\ell\in T$ has
$f_\ell$ odd, hence $f_\ell\ne0$, hence $|f_\ell|\ge N$, so $n+2\ge N$. Thus for $m<N-2$
every term of $H_m$ contains a vanishing factor. For $m=N-2$ the only possibility is a
single factor with $n=N-2$, and its $N$ face words all traverse $\ell$ in the same
direction. Two distinct plaquettes share at most one link, so any other link of a plaquette
occurring among the words is traversed only by the words of that plaquette, whose number
must be $0$ modulo $N$ and is at most $N$. Hence all $N$ words are the same oriented
plaquette $p$, and the element is
$\langle\chi_{\bar F}(U_p)|(-W)\mathcal R(-W)\cdots\mathcal R(-W)|\chi_F(U_p)\rangle$ with
$N-2$ insertions of $\chi_F(U_p)$. One-plaquette states are characters, multiplication by
$\chi_F$ adds a box, and the only chain of diagrams from $F$ to the column
$\bar F=\Lambda^{N-1}F$ is $F\to\Lambda^2F\to\cdots\to\Lambda^{N-1}F$, each step with
multiplicity one. The energy of $\chi_{\Lambda^k}(U_p)|0\rangle$ is
$2C_2(\Lambda^kF)=k(N-k)(N+1)/N$, so
$E_1-E_k=-(k-1)(N-k-1)(N+1)/N$ and
\[
  (-1)^{N-2}\prod_{k=2}^{N-2}\frac{1}{E_1-E_k}
  =-\Bigl(\frac{N}{N+1}\Bigr)^{N-3}\prod_{k=2}^{N-2}\frac{1}{(k-1)(N-k-1)}
  =-\Bigl(\frac{N}{N+1}\Bigr)^{N-3}\frac{1}{((N-3)!)^2} .
\]
The $\mathcal C$-odd diagonal element of an operator exchanging $\chi_F$ and $\chi_{\bar F}$
with amplitude $v$ is $-v$.
\end{proof}

The vertex is $-1$ at $N=3$, which is the first-order term $H_1=+I$ on the shell, and
$-25/144$ at $N=5$. Odd orders are therefore not the odd members of a series uniform in
$N$: they are determinant effects, present only when $N$ face words can close through
$\varepsilon$-tensors.

\begin{corollary}[flatness through third order]\label{cor:flat3}
For every $N\ge4$, $H_1$ and $H_3$ are scalars on the shell ($H_1=0$; $H_3=0$ except for
$N=5$, where it is $\tfrac{25}{144}I$). Hence through third order the bands are those of
Figure~\ref{fig:bands} and $\ker\partial_2$ is exactly flat.
\end{corollary}

\begin{result}[$\SU(3)$, third order]\label{res:third}
For $N=3$, exact evaluation of $H_3$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:su3}
  H_{\mathrm{eff}}=E_{\mathrm{flat}}(u)\,I+\Bigl(\frac{5}{612}u^2+\frac{1975}{124848}u^3\Bigr)\Ldn+O(u^4),
  \qquad
  E_{\mathrm{flat}}=\frac83+u+\frac{11}{306}u^2-\frac{109151}{249696}u^3 ,
\end{equation}
so the band is flat through third order for $\SU(3)$ as well. The third-order operator was
evaluated with two independent implementations, and its one-plaquette contributions with a
third, character-based computation; the $\Gamma$-point coefficients agree with the series
of \cite{Hamer1989}.
\end{result}

\section{Fourth order}\label{sec:fourth}

\subsection{Assumptions}

The fourth-order statements are exact rational computations. They rest on the following,
which we state once.

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:four}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item[(A1)] Since $PWP=0$ for $N\ge4$, the Hermitian fourth-order operator is
  $H_4=PW\mathcal RW\mathcal RW\mathcal RWP-\tfrac12\{PW\mathcal R^2WP,PW\mathcal RWP\}$.
  For $N=3$ the terms containing $PWP$ are included.
\item[(A2)] On the words reached at fourth order, the eigenspace of $H_E$ at energy $2\CF$
  is exactly $\mathcal P$. This was verified on all $224$ components encountered; it is not
  derived.
\item[(A3)] The connected-cluster decomposition is exhausted by the list: pairs, two-hop
  chains, fans, corners, and the two cube completions. This rests on a counting argument.
  The pair cluster is not computed over $\Q(N)$; its cancellation from the off-axis
  coefficient rests on a parity argument that we have not formalised.
\item[(A4)] For the closed forms in $N$, resolvents are taken at generic rank. The
  specialisation to integer $N$ is justified by argument for $N\ge5$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:faithful}); at $N=3$ and $N=4$ the closed forms are regular and
  agree with direct computation at that rank, as a checked fact.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

All all-rank computations were performed with one implementation, a Wilson-loop calculus
over $\Q(N)$ with Weingarten functions as symbolic Gram inverses; at $N=3$ the central
values were confirmed by two further independent implementations. An independent
recomputation of the corner cumulant at some $N\ge5$ is the most useful outstanding check.

\subsection{The kernel is a Hodge polynomial plus one operator}

Let $R$ be the cross-plane part of $\Ssq$: on a Bloch fibre, $R_{ij}=-w_i\bar w_j$ for
$i\ne j$ and $R_{ii}=0$.

\begin{result}[Hodge form]\label{res:hodge}
For $N=3$, on all $189$ translation classes of matrix elements of $H_4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hodge}
  H_4=-\tilde\nu\,(\Lup-2)+\omega\,\Ssq^2-\tilde\pi\,\Ssq+\tilde\sigma\,I-2C_{\shp}\,R ,
  \qquad \tilde\nu=-\frac{5}{48},
\end{equation}
exactly, where $\omega$ is the two-hop weight of Section~\ref{sec:closed} and
$\tilde\pi,\tilde\sigma$ are rational numbers. In particular the two-hop sector enters as
the \emph{square} of the second-order operator, and $R$ is the only operator outside the
algebra generated by $\Ldn$ and $\Lup$. The on-site amplitude $\tilde\sigma$ has not been
checked independently; it enters only the constant $\epsilon_\Gamma$ below.
\end{result}

\begin{proposition}[the complement of the flat band]\label{prop:feshbach}
On a fibre with $q>0$ let $\psi=w$. Then $\C^3=\ker\Ldn\oplus\ker\Lup$, the projector onto
$\psi^\perp$ is $Q_\psi=\Ldn/q$, and $Q_\psi\Ldn\psi=Q_\psi\Lup\psi=0$. Thus no polynomial
in $\Ldn$ and $\Lup$ couples the flat band to the dispersive doublet; in \eqref{eq:hodge}
only $R$ does.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:bloch} gives $\Ldn\psi=0$, $\Lup\psi=q\psi$, and $\Lup=0$ on $\psi^\perp$.
\end{proof}

The most general energy of the flat band allowed by cubic symmetry at this order is, with
$e_2=\sum_{i<j}a_ia_j$ and $e_3=a_1a_2a_3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ansatz}
  q\,\varepsilon_4(k)=\epsilon_\Gamma\,q+A_{\shp}\,q^2+4C_{\shp}\,e_2+B_{\shp}\,q\,e_2+D_{\shp}\,e_3,
  \qquad \varepsilon_4=\frac{\langle\psi,H_4\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle} .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[collapse of the cubic tier]\label{thm:collapse}
Let $\sigma(X)=\langle\psi,X\psi\rangle$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symbols}
  \sigma(I)=q,\quad \sigma(\Ssq)=-4q,\quad \sigma(\Ssq^2)=16q,\quad \sigma(\Lup)=q^2,\quad
  \sigma(R)=-2e_2 .
\end{equation}
Consequently any operator of the form \eqref{eq:hodge} has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BD}
  B_{\shp}=D_{\shp}=0,\qquad A_{\shp}=-\tilde\nu,\qquad
  \epsilon_\Gamma=\tilde\sigma+2\tilde\nu+16\omega+4\tilde\pi .
\end{equation}
Moreover $\sigma(R^2)=q\,e_2+3e_3$ and $\sigma(\Lup R)=-2q\,e_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first four symbols follow from $\Ldn\psi=0$ and $\Lup\psi=q\psi$. For $R$,
$\sigma(R)=-\sum_{i\ne j}|w_i|^2|w_j|^2=-2e_2$. Next $(R\psi)_i=-w_i(q-a_i)$, so
$\sigma(R^2)=\sum_ia_i\sum_{j\ne i}a_j(q-a_j)=2qe_2-(qe_2-3e_3)$, and
$\sigma(\Lup R)=q\,\sigma(R)$.
\end{proof}

The last two identities delimit the mechanism. The vanishing of $B_{\shp}$ and $D_{\shp}$
is not a consequence of the number of insertions of $R$: the single-$R$ operator $\Lup R$
would populate $B_{\shp}$. It is a consequence of the fact that $\Lup R$ does not occur at
fourth order. If $R^2$ occurs at sixth order, it contributes to the cubic tier in the fixed
ratio $D_{\shp}=3B_{\shp}$.

\subsection{Closed forms over \texorpdfstring{$\Q(N)$}{Q(N)}}\label{sec:closed}

\begin{result}[cumulants]\label{res:closed}
Computed over $\Q(N)$, every connected fourth-order cumulant in both $\mathcal C$ sectors is
a single rational function of $N$ (the pair cluster excepted, see
Theorem~\ref{thm:beta}). The two-hop weight, the single-contact dressing and the corner
dressing are
\begin{align}
  \omega_N&=\frac{N^3(N^2-4)^2\,p_{14}(N)}
      {2(N^2-1)^3(4N^2-9)^3(9N^2-16)(2N^2-3)(2N^2-1)^3(3N^2-2)\,r_2(N)},\label{eq:omega}\\
  d^{\,\mathrm s}_N&=\frac{2N^3(N^2-4)(10N^2-13)}{(N^2-1)^3(4N^2-9)^2(2N^2-1)^2},\label{eq:ds}\\
  d^{\,\mathrm c}_N&=\frac{16N^3(N^2-4)\,p_{18}(N)}
      {(N^2-1)^3(4N^2-9)^3(4N^2-1)(9N^2-25)(2N^2-1)^3(3N^2-1)(4N^2-5)\,r_4(N)},\label{eq:dc}
\end{align}
with $r_2(N)=(4N^2-N-4)(4N^2+N-4)$, $r_4(N)=(4N^2-2N-5)(4N^2+2N-5)$ and $p_{14},p_{18}$ as in
Appendix~\ref{app:poly}; the cube completions between opposite and between adjacent faces
are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubes}
  \kappa^{\mathrm{opp}}_N=-\frac{160}{N(N^2-1)^3},\qquad
  \kappa^{\mathrm{adj}}_N=-\frac{106}{N(N^2-1)^3} .
\end{equation}
Each form specialises to the value computed directly at rank $N$ for every
$N=3,\dots,70$. Every denominator factor is the energy denominator of an identified
intermediate state; all $21{,}556$ resolvent eigencomponents were verified to be exact
eigenvectors of every single-link Casimir over $\Q(N)$.
\end{result}

\begin{proposition}[specialisation]\label{prop:faithful}
The symbolic and fixed-rank computations can differ only through rank-dependent selection
rules, the Weingarten function, and the spectral decomposition. No word at fourth order
carries flux of magnitude at least $5$ on every excited link, and every Haar integral
involves at most three pairs of matrix elements per link; so for $N\ge5$ the selection rules
and the Weingarten inverse are the specialisations of the symbolic ones. The resolvent
denominators have exactly one integer root with $N\ge4$: at $N=4$, where the state with
three fundamental fluxes on one plaquette becomes $\bar F$ and is degenerate with the
shell. Hence for $N\ge5$ the closed forms are the fixed-rank values by argument.
\end{proposition}

The adjacent-face cube completion deserves a remark because it is the easiest term to get
wrong. The four remaining faces of the cube can be inserted in $4!=24$ orders. In units of
$1/1536$ at $N=3$, the orders have weights $-8$ (eight orders), $-4$ (six), $-2$ (eight)
and $-1$ (two), with total $-106/1536=-53/768$, in agreement with \eqref{eq:cubes}. An
enumeration that retains only some of the orders produces a wrong off-axis coefficient
while leaving all axial and $\Gamma$-point quantities unchanged, because $e_2=0$ on the
axes; off-axis checks are therefore essential. (An earlier enumeration in this project
held $8$ of the $24$ orders and gave $C_{\shp}$ too negative by $25/1024$.)

Put $\beta_N=8A_{\shp}+16C_{\shp}$. In the $\mathcal C$-odd sector the computed coplanar
dressings are the exact negatives of the perpendicular ones, the fan clusters cancel, and
under (A3) the assembly of the off-axis coefficient from the cumulants of Computed
result~\ref{res:closed} reduces to the left side of \eqref{eq:beta}.

\begin{theorem}[$\beta_N$ from three cumulants]\label{thm:beta}
As rational functions of $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
  -16\,\omega_N+32\,d^{\,\mathrm s}_N-16\,d^{\,\mathrm c}_N+\frac{848}{N(N^2-1)^3}
        =\frac{P_{17}(N^2)}{N\,R_{20}(N^2)} ,
\end{equation}
with $P_{17}$ and $R_{20}$ as in Appendix~\ref{app:poly}; under (A3) this is $\beta_N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After clearing denominators this is a polynomial identity in $N$. It has been checked in
the Lean~4 proof assistant (by the \texttt{ring} tactic) and, independently, in exact
arithmetic at the integers $N=3,\dots,200$, more points than the degree of the cleared
identity.
\end{proof}

For $N=3$,
\[
  \omega_3=\frac{360421351}{40327601932800},\qquad
  C_{\shp}=-\frac{13035490122347}{550663802582400},\qquad
  \beta_3=\frac{15644916262153}{34416487661400},
\]
that is, $C_{\shp}=-0.0236723\ldots$ and $\beta_3=0.4545762\ldots$.

\subsection{Dispersion, extrema and bandwidth}

Put $X_i=1-\cos k_i=a_i/2\in[0,2]$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
  \alpha_N=4A_{\shp}=-4\kappa^{\mathrm{opp}}_N=\frac{640}{N(N^2-1)^3} .
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:ansatz} and \eqref{eq:BD},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dispersion}
  \varepsilon_4(k)-\epsilon_\Gamma=A_{\shp}\,q+\frac{4C_{\shp}\,e_2}{q}
   =\frac{\alpha_N\sum_iX_i^2+\beta_N\sum_{i<j}X_iX_j}{2\sum_iX_i} .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positivity}
For every integer $N\ge3$, $\beta_N>\alpha_N>0$. As $N\to\infty$, $N^7\alpha_N\to640$ and
$N^7\beta_N\to6170/9$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All real roots of $R_{20}$ are at most $25/9$, so $R_{20}(z)>0$ for $z\ge9$. The numerator
of $N R_{20}(N^2)(\beta_N-\alpha_N)$ is $G(z)=P_{17}(z)-640R_{20}(z)/(z-1)^3$ at $z=N^2$, a
polynomial of degree $17$ with positive leading coefficient; isolating its real roots in
exact arithmetic shows that all are smaller than $9$. The same holds for $P_{17}$, whose
eleven real roots lie in $(0,2.19)$. The limits are ratios of leading coefficients.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[extrema]\label{thm:extrema}
Assume the form \eqref{eq:dispersion}. For every $N\ge3$ the function
$\varepsilon_4-\epsilon_\Gamma$ is positive for $k\ne\Gamma$, tends to $0$ as $k\to\Gamma$,
and attains its maximum exactly at $R=(\pi,\pi,\pi)$, where it equals $\alpha_N+\beta_N$. On
a coordinate axis it equals $\alpha_NX/2$. The fourth-order bandwidth of the flat band is
therefore
\[
  \Delta_4(N)=(\alpha_N+\beta_N)\,u^4,\qquad
  \Delta_4(3)=\frac{29985119454403}{34416487661400}\,u^4 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $f=(\alpha\sum X_i^2+\beta e)/(2s)$ with $s=\sum X_i$, $e=\sum_{i<j}X_iX_j$. With
$t=X_2+X_3$,
\[
  2s^2\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial X_1}
  =\alpha X_1^2+2\alpha X_1t+(\beta-\alpha)(X_2^2+X_3^2)+\beta X_2X_3 ,
\]
which by Lemma~\ref{lem:positivity} is positive on $[0,2]^3\setminus\{0\}$. So $f$ is
strictly increasing in each variable and its maximum on the cube is attained only at
$X=(2,2,2)$, where $f=(12\alpha+12\beta)/12$. Positivity of $f$ and its limit at the origin
are evident.
\end{proof}

For $N=3$ the theorem rests on Computed result~\ref{res:hodge}. For $N\ge4$ the form
\eqref{eq:dispersion} rests on (A3) and on the vanishing of $B_{\shp}$ and $D_{\shp}$,
which has been verified by direct computation at $N=4,5,6$ and at the larger ranks
examined.

\subsection{Coupling to the dispersive doublet}

\begin{theorem}[one angular invariant]\label{thm:mixing}
Let $p=\psi/\sqrt q$ and $x_i=a_i/q$. For $H_4$ of the form \eqref{eq:hodge},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixing}
  \|Q_\psi H_4\,p\|^2=4C_{\shp}^2\,q^2\,V(x),\qquad
  V(x)=\sum_ix_i^3-\Bigl(\sum_ix_i^2\Bigr)^2=\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j(x_i-x_j)^2\ \ge0 .
\end{equation}
$V$ vanishes exactly when the nonzero $x_i$ are equal: on the axes, the face diagonals and
the body diagonals, where the flat-band vector is an exact eigenvector of $H_4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:feshbach}, $Q_\psi H_4p=-2C_{\shp}Q_\psi Rp$. From
$(R\psi)_i=-w_i(q-a_i)$ and $\sigma(R)=-2e_2$,
$(Q_\psi R\psi)_i=w_i(a_i-q+2e_2/q)=q\,w_i\bigl(x_i-\sum_jx_j^2\bigr)$, using
$2e_2=q^2(1-\sum_jx_j^2)$. Hence
$\|Q_\psi R\psi\|^2=q^3\sum_ix_i(x_i-\sum_jx_j^2)^2=q^3V(x)$.
\end{proof}

Since the doublet lies $t_Nu^2q$ above the flat band, second-order perturbation theory in
this coupling contributes $-(4C_{\shp}^2/t_N)\,q\,V(x)\,u^6$ to the flat-band energy; for
$N=3$ the coefficient is $-0.2743614\ldots$. This is one contribution to the sixth order,
not the sixth order.

\section{One plaquette as free fermions}\label{sec:fermion}

\subsection{The representation}

Gauge-invariant functions of a single plaquette variable are class functions. After
multiplication by the Vandermonde determinant they become antisymmetric functions of the
eigenvalue angles $\theta_1,\dots,\theta_N$, and characters become Slater determinants
$|p_1,\dots,p_N\rangle$ of plane waves with momenta $p_j\in\Z+\tfrac{N-1}{2}$
\cite{GrossWitten1980,Wadia1980,MinahanPolychronakos1993,Douglas1993}. The trivial
representation is the Fermi sea $\{-\tfrac{N-1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{N-1}{2}\}$; a
representation with highest weight $\lambda$ has $p_j=\lambda_j-j+\tfrac{N+1}{2}$. The
Casimir formula reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:casimir}
  2C_2(\lambda)=\sum_jp_j^2-\frac{P^2}{N}-E_{\mathrm{sea}},\qquad
  P=\sum_jp_j,\qquad E_{\mathrm{sea}}=\frac{N(N^2-1)}{12},
\end{equation}
and multiplication by $\chi_F$ (respectively $\chi_{\bar F}$) raises (lowers) the momentum of
one fermion by one. For a single plaquette, whose four links carry the same representation,
the Hamiltonian \eqref{eq:H} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HSU}
  H_{\SU(N)}=H_{\U(N)}-\frac{P^2}{N},\qquad
  H_{\U(N)}=\sum_{j=1}^N\bigl(p_j^2-2u\cos\theta_j\bigr)-E_{\mathrm{sea}} ,
\end{equation}
that is, $N$ free fermions in a cosine potential with the centre-of-mass kinetic energy
removed. The doublet is $F=$ (sea with $\tfrac{N-1}{2}\to\tfrac{N+1}{2}$, $P=1$) and
$\bar F=$ (sea with $-\tfrac{N-1}{2}\to-\tfrac{N+1}{2}$, $P=-1$). For $\SU(N)$, Slater
determinants that differ by a common shift of all momenta are identified ($\det U=1$), and
\eqref{eq:HSU} is invariant under the shift. The shifted copy of $\bar F$ with $P=N-1$ is
$N-2$ steps from $F$; this is the vertex of Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}, and below order $N-2$
the identification plays no role. Let $K=\sum_mu^mK_m$ be the
effective Hamiltonian on the span of $F$ and $\bar F$. The splitting between the
$\mathcal C$-odd and $\mathcal C$-even one-plaquette energies is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sm}
  S(u)=\sum_mS_mu^m,\qquad S_m=-2\,K_m[F][\bar F].
\end{equation}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\U(N)$}{U(N)} doublet does not split}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nosplit}
For the Hamiltonian $H_{\U(N)}$ of \eqref{eq:HSU} the expansion of $K$ is well defined
through order $N-2$, and $K_m=\kappa_mI$ with a scalar $\kappa_m$ for every $m\le N-2$. In particular $K_m[F][\bar F]=0$ and $S_m=0$ for
$m\le N-2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$H_{\U(N)}$ is a sum of copies of the one-body operator $h=p^2-u(T+T\dg)$, where $T$ raises
the momentum by one, so its eigenvalues on antisymmetric states are sums of $N$ distinct
eigenvalues of $h$. At $u=0$ the levels $p^2$ of $h$ are degenerate in pairs $\{p,-p\}$, and
$h$ commutes with the reflection $p\mapsto-p$. The effective operator of $h$ on such a pair
is therefore of the form $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\ b&a\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, and
the coefficient of $u^m$ in $b$ is a sum over walks of length $m$ from $p$ to $-p$ with
steps $\pm1$, which requires $m\ge2|p|$. Hence the two eigenvalues emanating from the pair
satisfy $e_p^+-e_p^-=O(u^{2|p|})$.

At $u=0$ the level of $F$ is fourfold degenerate: a particle--hole excitation of the sea
costs at least $N$, with equality only when a fermion moves from $\pm\tfrac{N-1}{2}$ to
$\pm\tfrac{N+1}{2}$ (either sign for each), so the level consists of $F$, $\bar F$ and the
two states $D_\pm$ of total momentum $\pm N$. All four occupy every pair with
$|p|\le\tfrac{N-3}{2}$ fully and one member each of the pairs
$\mathsf A=\{\pm\tfrac{N-1}{2}\}$ and $\mathsf B=\{\pm\tfrac{N+1}{2}\}$. The four exact
eigenvalues emanating from this level are therefore $E_{\mathrm{in}}(u)+e_{\mathsf A}^{\pm}
+e_{\mathsf B}^{\pm}$, where $E_{\mathrm{in}}$ is the energy of the fully occupied inner
pairs, and they all lie within $O(u^{N-1})$ of their mean $E_0(u)$. The Hermitian effective
Hamiltonian $K^{(4)}$ on the fourfold level is analytic in $u$ and has these eigenvalues,
so $\|K^{(4)}-E_0(u)I\|=O(u^{N-1})$ and every Taylor coefficient of $K^{(4)}-E_0I$ of order
at most $N-2$ vanishes. Finally, $D_\pm$ is at least $N-1$ steps from $F$ and from
$\bar F$, so through order $N-2$ the states $D_\pm$ do not occur among the intermediate
states of the expansion on $\{F,\bar F\}$, and $K_m$ is the corresponding block of
$K^{(4)}_m$.
\end{proof}

Paths from $F$ to $\bar F$ do exist at low order; they cancel. At second order there are
two, through the sea and through the adjoint (both excitations present), with denominators
$+N$ and $-N$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:m2}
  K_2^{\U(N)}[F][\bar F]=\frac1N-\frac1N=0,\qquad
  K_2^{\SU(N)}[F][\bar F]=\frac{1}{N-\frac1N}-\frac{1}{N+\frac1N}=\frac{2N}{N^4-1},
\end{equation}
so that $S_2=-4N/(N^4-1)=-4N^{-3}+O(N^{-7})$ for $\SU(N)$ with $N\ge5$.

\subsection{Consequences for \texorpdfstring{$\SU(N)$}{SU(N)}}

By \eqref{eq:casimir} the $\SU(N)$ problem has the same states and matrix elements, and
each denominator $E_F-E_i$ of the $\U(N)$ expansion is replaced by
$E_F-E_i+(P_i^2-1)/N$, where $P_i$ is the momentum of the intermediate state.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:grading}
Let $m$ be even and $N\ge m+3$.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item $S_m^{\SU(N)}$ is exactly the difference between the expansion evaluated with
  denominators $E_F-E_i+(P_i^2-1)/N$ and with $E_F-E_i$: the splitting is generated
  entirely by the centre-of-mass term.
\item Decompose $S_m$ according to the first intermediate state. Every channel is
  $O(N^{-(m-1)})$, and the leading coefficients of the channels sum to zero.
\item $S_m^{\SU(N)}=O(N^{-(m+1)})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $N\ge m+3$ no determinant path contributes, so the $\SU(N)$ expansion is the $\U(N)$
expansion with shifted denominators, and (1) is Theorem~\ref{thm:nosplit}. An intermediate state with $n$ boxes and antiboxes has
$E_i-E_F=(n-1)N+O(1)$ by \eqref{eq:casimir}, and the states with $n=1$ are $F$ and
$\bar F$ themselves, which are excluded; so each of the $m-1$ denominators of a term is of
size $N$ and each channel is $O(N^{-(m-1)})$. The number of terms is independent of $N$
for $N\ge m+3$. The centre-of-mass correction is a relative $O(N^{-2})$ change of each
denominator, so the leading coefficient of each channel is its $\U(N)$ value, and these
sum to zero by Theorem~\ref{thm:nosplit}; this proves (2) and (3).
\end{proof}

Exact computation of $S_m$ over $\Q(N)$ for even $m\le10$ shows the following. For
$4\le m\le10$ exactly four channels contribute --- the singlet, the adjoint, and the two
two-box states --- with leading coefficients $(-4,+2,+1,+1)\cdot2^{m/2-2}$; at $m=2$ only
the singlet and adjoint occur, with $(-2,+2)$ as in \eqref{eq:m2}.
Corollary~\ref{cor:grading}(2) explains why these sum to zero. The computation shows more:

\begin{conjecture}[grading law]\label{conj:grading}
For every even $m$ and $N$ large enough, $S_m=c_mN^{-(2m-1)}\bigl(1+O(N^{-2})\bigr)$.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent
The computed values are $c_2=-4$, $c_4=-32$, $c_6=-1748/3$, $c_8=-123332/9$ and
$c_{10}=-49593808/135$. Corollary~\ref{cor:grading} gives the exponent $m+1$, which
coincides with $2m-1$ only at $m=2$. Writing the centre-of-mass term as $-\lambda P^2/N$,
$S_m$ is a polynomial in $\lambda$ without constant term, and the computation shows that
each coefficient of $\lambda^j$ with $1\le j\le m/2$ is separately $O(N^{-(2m-1)})$. What
remains to be proved is that for $j<m/2$ the $j$-fold insertion of $(P^2-1)/N$, whose
natural size is $N^{-(m-1)-2j}$, is in fact of this smaller order. The generic forms are
valid only for $N\ge m+3$; at smaller rank determinant effects of the kind
in Theorem~\ref{thm:parity} enter, and for instance the generic form of $S_4$ has a double
pole at $N=3$.

\begin{remark}[the same pattern in the band]
The closed forms of Sections~\ref{sec:second} and~\ref{sec:fourth} show the same
suppression for the full lattice problem: the second-order channels are $O(N^{-1})$ and
$t_N=O(N^{-3})$; the fourth-order channel terms are $O(N^{-3})$ and
$\alpha_N,\beta_N=O(N^{-7})$. In terms of the 't~Hooft coupling $\lambda_{\mathrm t}=g^2N$ one
has $u=N^2/\lambda_{\mathrm t}^2$, so a coefficient of order $N^{-(4k-1)}$ at order $u^{2k}$
gives a term $N\lambda_{\mathrm t}^{-4k}$ times a constant: every even order carries the same
single power of $N$. Whether this persists at sixth order for the multi-plaquette clusters
is open.
\end{remark}

value $\omega_3$, the identity \eqref{eq:beta} at $N=3,\dots,200$, and the $\SU(3)$

the polynomial identity in \eqref{eq:beta} has been formalised in a proof assistant.

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:grading}; a Slater--Condon evaluation in the exact
  orbital basis of $h$ reduces the $j=1$ term to a product of two one-particle matrix
  elements between adjacent orbitals, which obey the same power law numerically.
\item Remove (A2) and (A3) by an independent enumeration of the fourth-order support at
  some $N\ge5$, and recompute the corner cumulant there by a second method.
\item Determine whether $R^2$ occurs at sixth order, that is, whether the cubic tier
  appears with $D_{\shp}=3B_{\shp}$.
\item Bound the orders $m\ge5$ uniformly on a fibre, which would turn the fourth-order
  statements about the band into statements about $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
\end{enumerate}
